# Payday, title and personal loan complaints

Governing rules: Truth in Lending Act (APR disclosure), the Military Lending Act (36% MAPR cap for covered borrowers), and state small-dollar lending law, which varies widely.

Disclosures: the APR, finance charge, amount financed, and total of payments must have been disclosed before the consumer signed. A complaint that the cost was not clear is answered by resending the signed disclosure.

Repeat withdrawals: if two consecutive attempts to debit a payment fail, we may not attempt again without new written authorization from the consumer.

Payment plans: where state law provides an extended payment plan for payday loans, we offer it on request at no additional fee.

Title loans: the vehicle cannot be repossessed without the notices required by state law, and any surplus after sale is returned to the consumer.

Debt not owed: if the consumer says the loan was opened fraudulently, we freeze collection, request an identity theft report, and investigate within 30 days.

Response must: identify whether the complaint concerns cost/disclosure, withdrawals, repayment terms, or fraud; state the applicable step and timeline; and ask for the loan agreement date and the last four digits of the account.
